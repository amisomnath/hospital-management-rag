# Fever, Common Cold, Flu, and Respiratory Symptoms


> **Educational use only:** This file provides general health information. It is not a diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for care from a qualified clinician. In a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.


## Fever

Fever is an elevated body temperature, usually caused by infection but sometimes by other conditions. Temperature should be measured with a reliable thermometer rather than judged only by touch.

## Common cold

The common cold is usually a mild viral upper-respiratory illness. Typical features include:

- Runny or blocked nose.
- Sneezing.
- Mild sore throat.
- Mild cough.
- Mild tiredness.

## Influenza or flu

Flu symptoms often start more suddenly and may be more intense:

- Fever or chills.
- Cough.
- Sore throat.
- Runny or blocked nose.
- Muscle or body aches.
- Headache.
- Marked fatigue.
- Sometimes vomiting or diarrhoea, more often in children.

Symptoms alone cannot always distinguish flu, COVID-19, or another respiratory infection.

## General supportive care

- Rest.
- Drink fluids regularly.
- Use light clothing and a comfortable room temperature.
- Follow label directions for any over-the-counter medicine.
- Check with a clinician or pharmacist before giving medicines to children, pregnant people, older adults, or people with kidney, liver, heart, or stomach disease.
- Avoid antibiotics unless prescribed for a confirmed or suspected bacterial infection.
- Reduce spreading by washing hands, covering coughs, improving ventilation, and staying away from vulnerable people while acutely ill.

## Seek professional advice promptly when

- Fever persists, repeatedly returns, or is associated with worsening illness.
- The patient is pregnant, immunocompromised, very old, very young, or has serious chronic disease.
- There is ear pain, persistent sinus pain, worsening cough, or suspected pneumonia.
- Symptoms improve and then return more severely.
- A baby under 3 months has a fever.

## Emergency warning signs

- Difficulty breathing or fast breathing.
- Blue or grey lips or face.
- Chest pain.
- Confusion, seizure, inability to wake, or severe weakness.
- Severe dehydration or no urine.
- Coughing blood.
- A stiff neck with severe headache, light sensitivity, unusual rash, or altered consciousness.
- In children: ribs pulling in during breathing, no tears, very few wet nappies, inability to drink, or poor interaction.
