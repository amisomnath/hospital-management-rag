# Hypertension and Blood Pressure


> **Educational use only:** This file provides general health information. It is not a diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for care from a qualified clinician. In a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.


## What is blood pressure?

Blood pressure is the force of circulating blood against artery walls. A reading contains two numbers:

- **Systolic pressure:** pressure when the heart contracts.
- **Diastolic pressure:** pressure when the heart relaxes.

## What is hypertension?

Hypertension means blood pressure remains higher than the healthy range over time. A single elevated reading does not always confirm hypertension. Diagnosis usually requires properly taken repeated measurements, home monitoring, or ambulatory monitoring.

Thresholds can vary between clinical guidelines and individual situations. A clinician should interpret readings in context.

## Why it matters

High blood pressure often causes no obvious symptoms. Over time, it can damage the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes, and blood vessels and increase the risk of heart attack and stroke.

## Correct home measurement basics

- Avoid caffeine, smoking, and strenuous exercise for about 30 minutes beforehand.
- Empty the bladder.
- Sit quietly for at least 5 minutes.
- Keep the back supported and both feet flat on the floor.
- Support the arm at heart level.
- Use a validated upper-arm cuff of the correct size.
- Do not talk during measurement.
- Take two readings about one minute apart and record them.
- Measure at similar times when monitoring over several days.

## General risk-reduction measures

- Take prescribed medicine consistently.
- Reduce excess dietary salt.
- Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruits, pulses, whole grains, and appropriate protein.
- Maintain regular physical activity as medically appropriate.
- Aim for a healthy body weight.
- Avoid tobacco.
- Limit alcohol.
- Manage sleep problems and stress.
- Attend follow-up appointments.

## Do not

- Stop blood-pressure medicine because one reading is normal.
- Double a missed dose unless the prescriber or pharmacist advises it.
- Diagnose hypertension from headache, dizziness, or anxiety alone.
- Rely on wrist devices unless an appropriate validated upper-arm monitor cannot be used.

## Seek emergency care when

A very high reading is accompanied by chest pain, severe breathlessness, new weakness or numbness, facial droop, trouble speaking, confusion, seizure, fainting, sudden vision loss, or a severe unusual headache.

Pregnancy requires special caution. High readings with severe headache, vision changes, upper-abdominal pain, swelling, breathlessness, or reduced fetal movement need prompt obstetric assessment.
