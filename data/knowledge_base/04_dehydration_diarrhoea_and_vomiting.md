# Dehydration, Diarrhoea, and Vomiting


> **Educational use only:** This file provides general health information. It is not a diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for care from a qualified clinician. In a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.


## What is dehydration?

Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluid than it takes in. It may follow vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, heavy sweating, heat exposure, or inadequate fluid intake.

## Possible dehydration symptoms

- Thirst.
- Dry mouth, lips, or tongue.
- Dark yellow, strong-smelling urine.
- Passing urine less often.
- Dizziness or light-headedness.
- Headache.
- Tiredness.
- Sunken eyes.

Babies may have fewer wet nappies, few or no tears, a sunken soft spot, irritability, or unusual drowsiness.

## Oral rehydration

For fluid loss from diarrhoea or vomiting, an oral rehydration solution is often better than plain water because it replaces water and important salts in balanced amounts.

- Use a commercially prepared oral rehydration solution when available.
- Mix it exactly according to the packet instructions.
- Give small, frequent sips when nausea is present.
- Continue breastfeeding.
- Do not make the solution stronger or weaker than instructed.
- Avoid very sugary drinks, as they may worsen diarrhoea in some cases.

## Food

When the person can eat, offer small amounts of normal, easy-to-tolerate food. Prolonged fasting is usually unnecessary. Infants and children need age-appropriate feeding guidance.

## Preventing spread

- Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water.
- Clean contaminated surfaces.
- Avoid preparing food for others while ill.
- Do not share towels or utensils.
- Follow local guidance before returning to work, school, or food handling.

## Seek urgent medical advice when

- Vomiting prevents keeping fluids down.
- Diarrhoea is severe, prolonged, or associated with fever.
- There is blood in vomit or stool.
- There is severe or localised abdominal pain.
- The patient is an infant, pregnant, frail, elderly, immunocompromised, or has kidney/heart disease.
- Symptoms follow recent antibiotics, hospitalisation, or foreign travel.

## Emergency warning signs

- Confusion, collapse, or marked drowsiness.
- Very little or no urine.
- Severe weakness or inability to stand.
- Rapid breathing, racing heartbeat, cold or mottled skin.
- Severe abdominal pain.
- Vomiting blood or passing black stool.
- Signs of shock.
