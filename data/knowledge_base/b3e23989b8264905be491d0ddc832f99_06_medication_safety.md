# General Medication Safety


> **Educational use only:** This file provides general health information. It is not a diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for care from a qualified clinician. In a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.


## Core rules

- Use medicine only for the person for whom it was prescribed or purchased.
- Follow the prescribed dose, timing, duration, and route.
- Read the label and patient-information leaflet.
- Check the active ingredient to avoid accidentally taking duplicate medicines.
- Inform clinicians and pharmacists about allergies, pregnancy, breastfeeding, kidney disease, liver disease, stomach ulcers, bleeding disorders, and all other medicines or supplements.
- Store medicines away from children and pets.
- Do not use another person's prescription medicine.
- Do not crush, split, or open modified-release or enteric-coated products unless a pharmacist confirms it is safe.
- Do not stop long-term prescription medicine suddenly without professional advice.

## Missed doses

Instructions differ by medicine. Check the patient leaflet or ask a pharmacist. Do not automatically double the next dose.

## Antibiotics

- Antibiotics do not treat viral colds or flu.
- Use them only when prescribed.
- Take them exactly as directed.
- Do not save leftovers or share them.
- Report severe diarrhoea, rash, breathing difficulty, or swelling promptly.

## Pain-relief cautions

Over-the-counter pain medicines can cause serious harm when misused.

- Paracetamol/acetaminophen is present in many combination cold and flu products; duplicate dosing can damage the liver.
- Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs may not be suitable for people with kidney disease, ulcers, bleeding risk, certain heart conditions, or some forms of asthma.
- Aspirin is not appropriate for many children and teenagers unless specifically prescribed.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding require medicine-specific advice.

## Emergency reactions

Call emergency services for:

- Trouble breathing.
- Swelling of the lips, tongue, throat, or face.
- Collapse or fainting.
- Severe blistering or peeling rash.
- Suspected overdose.
- Severe confusion or seizure.

## Chatbot restrictions

The chatbot should not:

- Select a prescription medicine.
- Calculate a personalised prescription dose.
- Recommend changing or stopping prescribed treatment.
- Claim that a medicine is safe without considering age, pregnancy, allergies, conditions, and interactions.
- Replace a pharmacist, doctor, or poison-control service.
