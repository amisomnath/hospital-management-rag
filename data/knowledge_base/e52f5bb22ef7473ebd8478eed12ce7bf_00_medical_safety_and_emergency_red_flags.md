# Medical Safety and Emergency Red Flags


> **Educational use only:** This file provides general health information. It is not a diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for care from a qualified clinician. In a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.


## Purpose

This document helps a chatbot recognize situations that should not be handled only through self-care advice. The chatbot should clearly recommend urgent in-person medical evaluation when emergency warning signs are present.

## Call emergency services or go to an emergency department now when a person has

- Severe difficulty breathing, gasping, bluish or grey lips, or inability to speak normally because of breathlessness.
- New or severe chest pain, pressure, squeezing, or discomfort, especially with sweating, nausea, dizziness, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck, shoulder, or back.
- Sudden facial drooping, arm or leg weakness, difficulty speaking, confusion, loss of balance, or a sudden severe headache.
- Loss of consciousness, repeated fainting, a seizure, or inability to wake normally.
- Severe allergic-reaction signs such as swelling of the lips, tongue, face, or throat, wheezing, or trouble breathing.
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop with firm direct pressure.
- Vomiting blood, coughing blood, or passing black or visibly bloody stool.
- Severe dehydration with confusion, marked drowsiness, very little or no urine, or inability to keep fluids down.
- A serious injury to the head, neck, spine, chest, or abdomen.
- Suspected poisoning, overdose, or accidental ingestion of a harmful substance.
- Thoughts of self-harm, suicide, or harming another person.

## Important chatbot behaviour

1. Do not reassure the user that an emergency symptom is harmless.
2. Do not delay urgent care by asking many follow-up questions.
3. State that online advice cannot rule out a serious condition.
4. Encourage the person not to drive themselves when severely unwell.
5. For children, pregnancy, older adults, immunocompromised people, and people with major chronic illness, use a lower threshold for recommending professional care.
6. Do not prescribe prescription medicines or tell users to change doses.
7. Do not tell users to stop a prescribed medicine without speaking to the prescriber, unless emergency services instruct them.
