# Diabetes Overview


> **Educational use only:** This file provides general health information. It is not a diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for care from a qualified clinician. In a medical emergency, contact local emergency services immediately.


## What is diabetes?

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic condition in which blood glucose remains too high because the body does not make enough insulin, does not use insulin effectively, or both. Insulin helps glucose move from the blood into cells for energy.

## Main types

### Type 1 diabetes

The immune system damages the insulin-producing cells of the pancreas. People with type 1 diabetes require insulin treatment.

### Type 2 diabetes

The body becomes resistant to insulin and may gradually produce less insulin. Type 2 diabetes often develops slowly and may remain unnoticed for years.

### Gestational diabetes

This begins during pregnancy. It requires medical monitoring because it can affect both the pregnant person and the baby.

## Possible symptoms

- Increased thirst.
- Frequent urination.
- Unexplained weight loss.
- Increased hunger.
- Fatigue.
- Blurred vision.
- Slow-healing wounds.
- Recurrent infections.
- Tingling or numbness in the hands or feet.

Some people with type 2 diabetes have few or no obvious symptoms.

## Diagnosis

Diabetes cannot be confirmed from symptoms alone. Clinicians use blood tests such as fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c, or an oral glucose-tolerance test. Test selection and interpretation should be performed by a qualified healthcare professional.

## Long-term complications

Poorly controlled diabetes can damage blood vessels and nerves. Complications may affect the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes, feet, and peripheral nerves. Diabetes is associated with increased risks of heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, vision loss, foot ulcers, and amputation.

## General management principles

- Follow the personalised plan made with a clinician.
- Take prescribed medicines exactly as directed.
- Monitor glucose when advised.
- Eat balanced meals with attention to carbohydrate quantity and quality.
- Stay physically active within personal medical limits.
- Attend eye, kidney, foot, blood-pressure, and cholesterol checks.
- Avoid tobacco.
- Learn how to recognise and treat low blood glucose if using insulin or medicines that can cause hypoglycaemia.

## Possible low blood glucose symptoms

- Sweating.
- Shaking.
- Hunger.
- Palpitations.
- Dizziness.
- Irritability or confusion.
- Weakness.

Severe confusion, seizure, or unconsciousness is an emergency.

## Seek urgent medical care when

- There is severe vomiting, abdominal pain, deep or rapid breathing, confusion, fruity-smelling breath, or marked drowsiness.
- Blood glucose is very high and the person is acutely unwell.
- A person using diabetes medicine becomes unconscious or has a seizure.
- A foot wound is spreading, blackened, very painful, foul-smelling, or associated with fever.
